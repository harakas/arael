- **Residual scale audit for DOF / eigenvalue analysis**: The eigenvalue-based DOF calculation (`compute_dof` + friends) depends on the singular values of `J^T J` being well-separated from zero for real constraints and near-zero for truly redundant ones. Several residuals are deliberately multiplied by a sketch-scale factor (e.g. `arc.radius` on the sweep constraint, `mlen` on the parallel cross product) so their SVs track the sketch size and stay out of the DOF-noise floor even for very small or very large geometry. Constraints whose residuals are NOT scaled by a sketch-size quantity will produce naturally small singular values (think pure-angular residuals like `xangle` / `ArcLineParallel` / `ArcArcParallel`) and are candidates for false-positive DOF rejections on tiny sketches or false-negatives on huge ones. Concrete steps:
  1. Enumerate every `#[arael(constraint(...))]` in `arael-sketch-solver` and note, per residual row, whether it has a sketch-scale factor (radius, length, `mlen`, `constraint_isigma`-only, etc.).
  2. Build a test sketch in pathological scale regimes (1e-3 and 1e5 world units) and log the singular-value spectrum of `J^T J`. Flag residuals whose SV drops below the DOF-zero threshold at one scale but not the other.
  3. Specifically review the `xangle` (ArcRotation target) residual: it was originally `(arc.rotation - target) * arc.radius * isigma` to match the sweep pattern, dropped to `(arc.rotation - target) * isigma` in the v0.6.0 push because the radius factor let the solver collapse radius as a cheap way to zero out a distant target. The unscaled form might misbehave under eigenvalue-DOF at very large sketch scales. Concrete alternatives to try if it does, best-first:
     - **`(arc.rotation - target) * arc.radius_value * isigma`**. The `_value` suffix is the macro-synthesised shadow that reads the last-committed `.value` as a constant, with zero derivative in the generated Jacobian (same trick the SLAM drift regularizer uses for `pose.pos_value`). So this scaling: (i) tracks sketch size exactly like the old `* arc.radius` form, so the row's singular value stays at the usual scale; (ii) contributes zero gradient w.r.t. radius, so the solver *cannot* collapse radius to cheat on the rotation residual; (iii) the scaling factor evolves naturally across solves because `_value` updates from `.value` after each LM pass. Most likely the right answer -- recovers the DOF-floor benefit of the original formulation without its cost. Same move applies to `ArcArcParallel` if its pure `sin(a.rot - b.rot)` is too small at scale: multiply by `(arc_a.radius_value + arc_b.radius_value) / 2`.
     - `(arc.rotation - target) * min(arc.radius, arc.radius_b)`. Keeps the scale link but still leaves a non-zero radius gradient (just clamped to the smaller axis), so the collapse pressure is weaker but not eliminated.
     - An explicit soft ceiling on `|rotation - target|` that saturates above a tolerance so the solver can't trade radius for rotation regardless of scaling.
     Same review applies to ArcLineParallel (`... / mlen` -- uses the live `mlen`, same collapse risk at tiny line lengths; `mlen` could become a `_value`-pattern if needed) and ArcArcParallel (`sin(a.rot - b.rot)` -- pure dimensionless, no sketch-scale link at all).
  4. Document the convention in a short comment block above the constraint bodies so future contributors know why the radius / mlen factors are there and when to keep them.

- **arael-sym**: investigate a first-class `branch()`/piecewise construct instead of heaviside-multiplication. Multiplying by `heaviside(c)` compiles to a select (csel/cmov), never a real branch: LLVM cannot skip evaluating the "off" arm (both operands of the multiply are computed), and it cannot fold `0 * x` to 0 because x may be inf/NaN -- so `H(c) * f + (1 - H(c)) * g` always pays for BOTH arms and propagates NaN/inf out of the disabled one. A `branch(cond, then_expr, else_expr)` expression node could codegen an inline `if`, evaluate only one arm, stay NaN-safe, and differentiate piecewise (derivative of the selected arm, like clamp's pass-through philosophy). Would also give safe formulations of log-map-style residuals (Taylor fallback arm near the singularity) without epsilon biasing.

- **arael**: rotation-param deserialize/advance contract -- spans all three SO(3) primitives, with one shared part and one QuaternionParam-specific part. SHARED contract: `value` doubles as the serialize input (initial guess) and the deserialize output (final result), and the model is left in an intermediate state after a solve until `deserialize(&result.x)` syncs it. The live state during the solve sits in a different member per param: `work` for SimpleEulerAngleParam (advance is a no-op; `value` stays at the initial), `ref_rotation` for EulerAngleParam (advance folds the delta there; `value` is untouched mid-solve; deserialize sets `value = ref_rotation.get_euler_angles()`), and `value` itself for QuaternionParam (advance folds directly into it). So `deserialize(&result.x)` is MANDATORY to read the result for Simple and Euler (their `value` is stale otherwise) and only a near-no-op for Quaternion -- document this, and the clean fix makes the delta/working state fully solver-internal behind one absolute-orientation read-back so callers never see the intermediate state (apply the same read-back shape to all three so the contract is uniform). QuaternionParam-SPECIFIC retraction mismatch: `advance` and `__precompute` fold the rotation-vector delta via `from_rotation_vector_small` (the exp map it parameterizes) but `deserialize_params{32,64}` fold it via `from_euler_angles`. Dormant today -- the solver zeroes the delta on every accepted step, so deserialize always receives delta = 0 and both maps reduce to identity, zero numerical effect -- but wrong in principle, a latent trap if the solver were ever changed to hand back a nonzero rotation delta. EulerAngleParam does NOT share this: its advance, deserialize, and __precompute all fold via `rotation_from_euler_angles` (self-consistent); SimpleEulerAngleParam has no delta to fold. The deeper wart is the `value` conflation (serialize source / mid-solve accumulator / deserialize target). Options: (a) correct fix -- a bigger API change that makes the delta/reference split purely solver-internal: the caller hands in and reads back an absolute orientation, and the solver converts to a zero-centered delta at entry and back at exit with one consistent retraction. The external representation must NOT be euler angles (`get_euler_angles`/`from_euler_angles` degenerate at pitch = +-90 with ~sqrt(eps) recomposition error -- EulerAngleParam already eats this on its read-back, inherent to being the euler param, but routing QuaternionParam's result through euler would reintroduce exactly the gimbal-lock loss it exists to avoid). Prefer the ROTATION VECTOR (log map of the reference) as the 3-number external form: gimbal-free (singular only at angle = pi, not pi/2) and its exp/log IS the solver's own retraction (`from_rotation_vector_small` ~ exp map), so entry and exit use the same map as the inner loop -- fully consistent, no euler anywhere. Or keep the unit quaternion as the stored `value` with a clean read-back that returns what `advance` maintained. (b) semi-correct fix (QuaternionParam) -- deserialize folds into a SEPARATE member instead of `value` (or just derives `value` from the advance-maintained accumulator), dropping the euler retraction; keep the unit quaternion as the accumulator `advance` renormalizes -- do NOT make the raw matrix `ref_rotation` the accumulator, it drifts off SO(3) in f32. Numerical no-op today (delta is always 0 at deserialize), so pair it with a `debug_assert!(delta ~ 0)` that makes the dropped-delta case loud if the invariant ever breaks.

- **benchmarks/loc**: investigate why f32 loses to f64 on the Cortex-A76 specifically. At matched iteration counts f32 wins as expected on two of three cores -- markedly on a Pi Zero (ARM1176/ARMv6, VFPv2: 47.7 vs 65.0 ms/iter, ~27% faster) and marginally on the dev machine (aarch64 VM: 0.91 vs 0.93 at LOC_POSES=120) -- but on a Raspberry Pi 5 (Cortex-A76), also both 3(3) iterations, f32 is SLOWER than f64 (1.35 vs 1.28). So the A76 is the outlier. Candidates: f32 sqrt/division not faster than f64 on the A76 (it pipelines both well), NEON f32 not vectorizing in the band Cholesky or the Hessian assembly, or widening/narrowing at the solver boundary. Profile the band solve + assembly per precision on the A76; compare against pgo/slam to see whether it is band-solver specific. Confounder to control for: ms/iter = total_ms / iterations is skewed when a solve takes a cheap/rejected iteration -- that faked an f32 edge at 60/300 poses where f32 ran 2(3) vs f64 3(3). Match iteration counts (or measure a fixed-iteration steady state) before comparing precisions; consider reporting a per-iteration cost that excludes the outlier iteration.
